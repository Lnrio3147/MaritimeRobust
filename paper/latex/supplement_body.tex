\section{Appendix B. Margin-space superadditivity}
The original analysis measured superadditivity in margin space (difference between compound accuracy and the additive expectation of margin losses): haze+glare extra margin loss $-$0.152 … $-$0.032, haze+cloud $-$0.144 … +0.009, across four backbone families. It agrees in sign with the accuracy-space result reported in §4.3 and is not used for inference here.

\section{Appendix C. CIFAR-10-C arms: protocol, seeds and intervals}
All arms in §4.7 share one protocol: ResNet-18 with the CIFAR stem (3$\times$3, no max-pool), 200 epochs of SGD at learning rate 0.1, momentum 0.9, weight decay 5e-4, nesterov, cosine schedule, batch 128, 4-pixel pad-and-crop and horizontal flip. An arm that trains on degradations replaces the clean view with a corrupted one at probability 0.5 and leaves the geometry pipeline untouched; the clean reference sees clean images only. Every arm is evaluated on the same 601 conditions --- clean, 75 single, 525 pair --- on the full 10,000-image test set.

\textbf{Why raw accuracy and not mCE.} mCE normalizes each corruption's error by a fixed AlexNet baseline and aggregates only the fifteen single-corruption families, so it has no slot for pair conditions --- the object of this study --- and would place our numbers on a scale we did not measure. Arms are therefore compared only against the clean reference trained under the identical recipe, and no claim of parity with published CIFAR-10-C tables is made. The AugMix arm tests the mechanism claim (does generic augmentation substitute for family coverage), not a leaderboard position.

\textbf{AugMix predictions.} Three predictions were frozen before that arm ran. A1: AugMix lands near the singles arm (86.77\%) rather than the paired arms (87.88--88.52\%). A2: its held-out excess stays positive, unlike the coverage arms ($-$0.17, $-$0.42). A3: its clean accuracy does not fall below the clean reference's 95.47\%. A1 failed, and in the opposite direction from the prediction: AugMix reaches 78.72\%, 8.05 pp \emph{below} the singles arm. A2 held (+1.97 pp). A3 held to within 0.11 pp (95.36 vs 95.47). The failure is informative rather than awkward: the generic augmentation never targets the families under test, so it does not substitute for covering them, but it also does not approach the arm that shows those families one at a time.

The arm was repeated at a second seed to check that A1's margin is not seed noise. It is not: seed 29 gives 78.73\% on compounds against 78.72\% at seed 17 (gain over clean +21.44 pp, CI [+20.16, +22.73]), clean 95.85\%, singles 89.39\%, and the held-out excess stays positive (+2.44 pp at severity 3, against +1.97 pp at seed 17). A generic augmentation that never samples the evaluated families sits \textasciitilde{}9 pp below coverage at both seeds, so the +9.17 pp contrast is a property of the arms rather than of one training run --- the same reading as the coverage arms' own seed spreads of 0.10 pp and 0.19 pp.
